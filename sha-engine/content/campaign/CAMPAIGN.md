# Sundays — the full campaign

Sha is opening **Sunday 10am–2pm, every week, permanently.** First Sunday
**30 August 2026**.

---

## The idea the whole campaign rests on

Four hours is not a lot, and that is the asset.

| Service | Time |
|---|---|
| Blonde Transformation + K18 | **4 hrs — the entire window** |
| Balayage / Lived-in Blonde | 3 hr 30 |
| Partial (half) foil | 2 hr 30 |
| Root Colour + Refresh | 1 hr 45 |
| Blow Wave | 45 min |

**A Sunday holds one big colour. Occasionally two smaller things. Never more.**

That is a timetable, not a marketing device — which means every urgency line in
this campaign is literally true, and none of it needs a discount. Invariant 7
holds without effort here: the scarcity *is* the product. There is not a single
price anywhere in the nine assets. The story is time, and time is the thing she
is genuinely short of.

**Positioning line:** *Sunday might be the only four hours in your week that
nobody else has a claim on.* Her audience is working mums in Camberwell.
Weekdays are work, Saturday is sport and parties and errands. Sunday 10–2 is the
one block that is actually theirs. Sell that, not the haircut.

## The campaign mark

A shallow gold arc, ends dotted, labelled **10** and **2** — the arc of the day,
the window itself. It appears on all nine assets. Six posts, three stories and
an email read as one campaign rather than nine separate things, and no other
local salon is using a geometric time mark. That is most of why it will stand
out in a feed of before-and-afters.

## SMS drafts

**Compliance first, because this one has teeth.** Under the Australian Spam Act,
a commercial SMS needs consent, clear identification of the sender, and a
functional opt-out. Only send to clients who have given consent, and keep the
opt-out on every marketing message. Numbers 1–3 are marketing and carry it.
Number 4 is a genuine one-to-one message and does not need it. Number 5 is a
transactional appointment reminder, which is exempt.

**1 · Announcement — all consenting clients**
```
Hi [Name], it's Sha. I'm open Sundays now, 10–2, every week. Only four hours so it books out fast. hairbysha-booking.onrender.com/book — reply STOP to opt out.
```

**2 · Lapsed clients — the re-activation**
```
Hi [Name], it's Sha at Hair by Sha. It's been a while. I've opened Sundays 10–2, in case a weekday was the problem. Would love to get you back in. hairbysha-booking.onrender.com/book — reply STOP to opt out.
```

**3 · This Sunday, still free — the urgency send**
```
[Name], Sha here. Sunday's chair is still open — 30 Aug, 10–2. It's one appointment and first in gets it. hairbysha-booking.onrender.com/book — reply STOP to opt out.
```

**4 · One-to-one, for anyone who has ever said weekends are easier**
```
Hi [Name], it's Sha. You mentioned once that weekends were easier for you — I'm doing Sundays now, 10 til 2. Want me to hold one for you?
```
*This one will outperform the other three combined. It is personal, it references
a real conversation, and it asks a question rather than pointing at a link. Send
it by hand to ten people.*

**5 · Day-before reminder — transactional**
```
See you tomorrow at [time], [Name]. It's Sha — Prospect Hill Rd, Camberwell. Text this number if you're running late.
```

## Further strategy

### 1. Google Business Profile — the biggest single opportunity, by a distance

The July growth plan found she has **no Google Business Profile**, and called it
the largest discovery gap in the business. Sunday opening changes that from an
important job into an urgent one.

**"Hairdresser open Sunday near me" is a high-intent search with almost no
competition in the eastern suburbs**, because almost no salon opens Sunday.
Someone typing that is not browsing — they want an appointment today. A profile
with Sunday hours listed puts her in front of that search, and she would be one
of very few results.

That is a bigger prize than anything else in this campaign. It is free, it takes
about an hour, and it works every Sunday from here rather than once.

### 2. A "Sundays" page on her website

Same logic, second channel. One page targeting *hairdresser open Sunday
Camberwell* / *Sunday hair appointment Melbourne*. Low competition, high intent,
and it gives the Instagram bio link somewhere better to point during the launch.

### 3. The waitlist is a real thing, so use it

One chair means she will turn people away. Do not let that demand evaporate —
capture it. "Sunday's taken. Want the next one?" A waitlist is honest scarcity,
it makes the following Sunday feel contested, and it hands her a booked-out
month instead of a booked-out week.

### 4. Sunday is when people are free *together*

The only day her clients' friends are also free. Two blow waves fills half the
window, and one client bringing a friend is the cheapest new client she will
ever get. Worth testing as a story or a one-to-one ask — not as a discount.

### 5. Point the existing Facebook ads at Sunday

The two ads already built lead on a lived-in blonde and on the straight answer.
Swapping the headline to lead with Sunday availability is a fifteen-minute
change and gives the paid spend a genuine news hook, which is what cold audiences
respond to.

### 6. Film the first Sunday

Video runs 8.11% on this account against 1.11% for stills. The first Sunday is
the most filmable thing that will happen all month — an empty calm salon at 10am,
one client, the light. It costs a propped phone and nothing else.

## Measurement

Ask one question at the end of each Sunday: **did the four hours fill, and with
what?** A Sunday filled with a blonde transformation is a very different result
from a Sunday filled with one blow wave, and the campaign should be judged on
which of the two it produced.

If the first two Sundays fill with colour, the constraint becomes capacity and
the right conversation is a second Sunday chair or a longer window. If they fill
with blow waves, the offer is working but the positioning is pulling the wrong
service and posts 3 and 6 need to lean harder on the big colour.
